# Accessibility Screen Coverage

Ariadne routes Terraria interfaces through three accessibility layers. Purpose-built screens provide the richest semantics for common flows. A universal adapter supplies baseline keyboard and screen-reader access to every stock or mod-provided `UIState` that does not have a dedicated implementation. Small observers cover legacy overlays drawn directly by `Main`.

## Input Contract

- Up/Down, Home/End, and Page Up/Page Down navigate collections.
- Letter keys jump through controls by semantic name.
- Enter activates the primary action. Shift+Enter requests an alternate/right-click action where the live control supports one.
- Left/Right adjust sliders and choices. Hierarchical Ariadne screens also use Right to open and Left to return.
- Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab move sideways in the inventory tree to the next and previous sibling pane at the current depth, landing inside it at the position last used there. Siblings that hold no entries, such as the sort actions, stay out of that ring and remain reachable with Up/Down.
- Ctrl+R reads focused details and F1 reads contextual controls.
- Escape follows the current screen's native back/cancel behavior.

During gameplay, existing WASD bindings continue to move the player. The default independent aim bindings are O/K/L/Semicolon for up/left/down/right, I uses the held item, P performs secondary use or interaction, and Left Alt plus Move Left/Right cycles combat targets. Left Alt plus Move Down clears the target and recenters. Only the direction that issues a targeting command is consumed, and only on its press edge, so holding the chord continues to walk. Left Alt is Ariadne's command prefix generally: held with W it opens waypoints, with Apostrophe it toggles passive radar, and with Q or E it steps the selected hotbar slot backward or forward. A chord whose second key also carries a native binding, as E does for Grapple, withholds that trigger for as long as the chord is held; the keys are resolved from the live binding rather than named in code, so a rebind stays covered. These are ordinary rebindable Ariadne keybinds in Gameplay Keyboard Bindings. On the first load of this control set, Ariadne fills only missing keyboard assignments across tModLoader input profiles, saves them through `PlayerInput.Save`, and writes a versioned migration marker; existing non-empty bindings are preserved, and later rebinding or clearing is never forced back. A default that supersedes an earlier shipped default additionally re-points a profile whose only assignment for that control is still exactly the superseded key, so a moved default reaches a profile that already received the old one; any other assignment, including one that merely adds a second key, is a deliberate choice and is left alone. Terraria's existing Smart Cursor binding and toggle-versus-hold preference remain authoritative. F1 reads the current gameplay bindings and precedence rules.

## Spatial Boundaries

The virtual world cursor owns ordinary gameplay aim without moving the operating-system pointer or changing Terraria's input mode. Precision mode retains a world tile, reports every manual tile step—including held repeats and adjacent identical tiles—with its semantic target, tile coordinates, and native-style item reach. Coordinates are enabled by default; disabling them restores the target, reach, and player-relative description. Coordinates themselves are spoken as an offset from the player, who counts as zero, because raw world tile numbers reach the thousands on a large world; a configuration option restores raw numbers, and waypoint names and scanner results keep raw coordinates either way so a saved name or a fixed snapshot stays meaningful after the player moves. Every foreground-tile or liquid step also plays the sound Terraria associates with that tile or liquid; declared variants cycle in order, liquid takes precedence over an underlying tile, and a new step replaces unfinished cursor audio. Ariadne asynchronously warms common sounds from the player's installed Terraria content, caches decoded PCM only in memory, and applies its shared ILD, ITD, and vertical-pitch transform. An uncached, modded, resource-pack, or unsupported asset falls back immediately to Terraria's ordinary playback rather than blocking cursor input. Empty space—including wall- or wire-only coordinates—is silent. Smart Cursor projects the retained direction toward the viewport edge, suppresses empty-space feedback, and reports every changed resolved coordinate or same-tile semantic state with a native earcon and only the concise target name—never coordinates or reach—while leaving Terraria's mining, placement, wiring, and painting resolver intact. Switching modes announces Smart cursor or Unlocked cursor first; entering unlocked mode starts the precision cursor on the player's chest tile using the player's world position, including when the camera is offset near a world edge. An immediately resolved smart target is queued behind the Smart cursor phrase. Background walls, including unsafe natural walls, are ignored unless the held item has hammer power. Combat targeting includes only active, damageable, lit, on-screen enemies with native-style line of sight, treats `realLife` multipart enemies as one cycling entry, speaks the selected entity with current/maximum HP, and plays a separate screen-positioned confirmation cue through the hostile-audio enabled state and volume. Wall, hostile/combat-target, and cursor audio keep independent enabled states and volumes. The global ITD switch and maximum-delay strength apply to all cached or Ariadne-authored spatial audio; fallback native cursor sounds retain Terraria's ordinary spatial pan. Inventory, chat, dialogs, maps, scanner states, pause, focus loss, death, housing placement, camera capture, and other focused UI suspend coordinate ownership, restore the sampled physical pointer, and stop cursor audio.

The radar shares the scanner's capture and therefore its `Lighting.Brightness > 0` rule, so it is not a through-wall or through-darkness detector either: an unlit cave is silent, and anything the radar pings is something the scanner will list. It measures its own range as a radius from the spatial observer rather than from the camera, kept short enough to stay inside both the fixed spatial field and the screen at default zoom. Discovery is change-driven and remembers what it has already reported; a tile group is known once any one of its tiles is remembered, which keeps a vein from being rediscovered as more of it is lit, while creatures and dropped items are remembered by their slot-reuse-safe identity and forgotten after roughly ten seconds out of range. Remembered tiles are dropped once they fall well past the radar's reach, so leaving an area and returning reports it again. A sweep that cannot be heard—because a menu, chat, pause, or focus loss holds the listener—still updates that record and sounds nothing, so closing the screen never empties a backlog. Discovery is capped per sweep and spaced so two pings cannot overlap; anything held back stays unreported until a later sweep reaches it rather than being silently consumed.

The visible-surroundings scanner remains deliberately limited to the current zoom-aware camera viewport and current `Lighting.Brightness > 0`; it is not a persistent explored-world or through-wall detector. Its immutable snapshot includes only lit evidence, so hidden portions of veins and pools do not contribute to their reported sizes. Close and scan again to refresh it. Players, projectiles, wires, a separate trap category, ordinary terrain blocks, and background walls are not included. A selected target can still become stale, and teleport safety avoids collision, dangerous tiles, lava, shimmer, and unsupported falls but does not protect against nearby enemies.

NPC housing selection still opens Terraria's native world-placement cursor after the NPC or query is chosen, and camera capture mode remains outside virtual-cursor ownership. Tile-by-tile map imagery also remains outside this feature. These surfaces need separate semantic placement or capture workflows; they are not silently classified as accessible menus.

The universal adapter intentionally favors semantic UI state over rendered pixels. A newly encountered control should first be fixed by exposing a useful localized label or supported action in the adapter. A purpose-built state is appropriate when the live screen depends on visual layout, has compound actions, or needs transaction/cancel semantics that a generic click cannot communicate safely.
